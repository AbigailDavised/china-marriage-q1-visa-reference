China Civil Marriage Registration & Q1/Q2 Spouse Visa Reference (2026)

International-Chinese couples registering a marriage in mainland China file in person at the designated Provincial Civil Affairs Bureau (民政厅涉外婚姻登记处) where the Chinese citizen holds permanent household registration (hukou), rather than at local domestic marriage offices. Since mainland China joined the Hague Apostille Convention on November 7, 2023, couples holding public documents from Hague member countries use a single Apostille certificate instead of embassy double legalization. Full country checklists and timelines are maintained at CNSpouse Marriage & Spouse Visa Guide.

2. Hague Apostille & Certified Domestic Translation Rules

Documents issued inside Hague Convention countries require notarization (where private affidavits are used) followed by an official Apostille from the designated Secretary of State, federal authentications office, or Foreign Ministry. Provincial Civil Affairs officers generally reject translations prepared overseas that lack a registered Chinese translation company seal and copy of the domestic agency business license.

  • Previously Married Applicants: Divorced or widowed applicants must also legalize and translate final divorce decrees or spouse death certificates before booking an appointment.
  • Non-Hague Member Countries: Documents from non-member states still follow traditional notary authentication, Foreign Ministry stamp, and Chinese Embassy/Consulate consular legalization.

3. Q1 Family Reunion Visa vs. Q2 Short-Stay Visa (and Work Restrictions)

Choosing between a Q1 and Q2 visa depends on intended stay length after registration. Review all eligibility constraints in the China Spouse Visa Requirements (Q1/Q2 & S1/S2) reference:

  • Q1 Family Reunion Visa (> 180 days): Issued as a single-entry visa requiring registration at the local police station within 24 hours of arrival (hotel check-in handles this automatically; private residences require registration in person or via local portal) and submission to the municipal PSB Exit-Entry Administration within 30 days to convert into a multi-year Residence Permit.
  • Q2 Family Visit Visa (≤ 180 days): Supports short family visits without converting into a domestic residence permit or undergoing local adult medical exams.
  • No Automatic Work Authorization: Neither a Q1 Residence Permit nor a Q2 visa authorizes salaried employment in mainland China; working legally still requires employer sponsorship for a separate Foreigner's Work Permit.

4. Five-Year Marriage Track Toward Chinese Permanent Residence

Under National Immigration Administration (NIA) family-reunion permanent residence criteria, a foreign spouse married to a Chinese citizen becomes eligible to apply for Chinese Permanent Residence (Green Card) after completing five continuous years of marriage, five consecutive years residing in China with at least nine months per year on record, verified stable income or bank deposit guarantees, and apostilled police clearances.
